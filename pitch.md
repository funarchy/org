# The Two-Minute Pitch

The spoken first-contact narrative — part of the solution to
pollution #4. This pitch is a filter, not a persuasion: it selects
the people who already feel the fire. When a skeptic pushes back
instead, the armor lives in [faq.md](faq.md). The written companion
is the one-pager (pending).

~350 words. Speak it, don't send it.

---

The world of software is on fire, and everyone is pretending it's
weather.

Look at any software company. Its tracker overflows with problems —
written by users, begging, thousands sitting open for years. Now
find the problem the company itself committed to solve — in
writing, with a way to tell when it's solved. It isn't there.
Companies promise features and ship checkmarks; the problem is
never promised, and never answered. And everything else follows
from that. Work starts inspired and goes closed; motivation turns
unclear even to the people inside. Users aren't earned, they're
bought — the industry now pays two dollars in sales and marketing
for every dollar of new revenue it buys. And the story ends one of
two ways: the exit, or the shutdown. Sold out from under its
users — or gone like InVision: a two-billion-dollar valuation, shut
down, nothing left. Either way, it's the escape plan of people
their own company burned out.

I decided to skip both endings. Instead of getting rich first and
then doing what I love, I'm doing what I love now — paid fairly,
built to last, for as long as I can.

Funarchy is what that decision looks like as an organization: a
workers' cooperative where every piece of work starts from a
written problem, in public. Problems, solutions, finances,
decisions — one public record anyone can read or fork. Members are
paid at or above market; surplus goes back into the work; and there
is no exit — a cooperative can't be sold out from under the people
it serves, because no one owns it to sell.

Every piece of this is proven somewhere: Ghost can't legally be
sold. Buffer publishes every salary. Blender answers to no
shareholder. What doesn't exist is all of it in one place. That's
Funarchy.

We build the tools that make this way of working possible: a
development environment teams own instead of rent by the seat —
ask anyone who lived through Unity's runtime fee — and a
problem-first process layer where every change must answer a
written problem, for humans and their AI agents alike.

Marketing budget: zero. The public record is the landing page.

And "as long as I can" has a design behind it: because everything
is public, the day I can't — someone else can.

Three doors, pick yours. Use what we build. Contribute —
contribution is the path to paid membership. Or fund it — or
introduce me to someone who needed to hear this.

## Delivery notes

- The fire is one enemy with three faces (closed work, bought
  users, the exit), all rooted in "the problem is never promised."
  If you only land one sentence, land that one. Never say "nobody
  writes down problems" — users write them constantly; the trackers
  are full of them. The claim is that no company commits to one and
  reports back whether it got solved.
- Every number in the pitch is sourced and survives pushback:
  - "$2 per $1": 2025 B2B SaaS median — companies spend a median of
    $2 in sales and marketing to acquire $1 of new ARR
    (Benchmarkit). In pocket for the skeptic: Asana FY2025 —
    $420M on sales & marketing against $724M revenue, $255M net
    loss.
  - InVision: raised ~$350M, valued at $2B, revenue halved to $50M
    by 2022, shut down end of 2024. Nobody exited rich.
  - In pocket, never volunteered: Godot's proposal tracker has
    6,400+ open proposals, and each is *required* to state its
    problem before the feature — proof that users write problems
    and companies leave them open, not that problems go unwritten.
- The lineage sentence ("proven somewhere... all of it in one
  place") is a preemptive block. Without it, the first gamedev
  listener says "Blender already does this." Never claim novelty on
  any single piece — Ghost (unsellable non-profit, live financials),
  Buffer (open salaries since 2013), Blender/Godot (foundations),
  Igalia (worker co-op, equal pay) each own a piece. The claim is
  the combination.
- Unity's runtime fee is the gamedev-native proof of "own, don't
  rent": announced Sept 2023, developer revolt within a week, CEO
  gone within a month, fee cancelled a year later — because a
  company that owns the engine could change the terms retroactively.
  One clause in the pitch; the full story stays in pocket.
- The turn must arrive by ~20 seconds. The rant earns attention;
  the turn spends it.
- "Paid fairly" in the turn is load-bearing — without it the line
  reads "love instead of money" and re-arms the nobody-works-unpaid
  objection ([faq.md](faq.md)).
- If someone says "antifragile?" — the answer is already in the
  pitch: public record, forkable, "the day I can't — someone else
  can." Mechanism, never the term.
- Three doors are a menu: offer the one that fits the listener,
  keep the others in pocket.
- Business-skeptic pushback (charity? funding? displacement? CAC?)
  — don't improvise, the sentences are written in [faq.md](faq.md).
