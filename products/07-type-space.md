# Type Space

**A keyboard-mastery game that grows into a cooperative creativity tool -
where ideas move from head to world at the speed of typing, and the
finished work shows which parts are human.**

## What it is

Three stages, one product.

**Stage one: a typing game that is actually a game.** The keyboard is one
of the most effective mechanisms a person has for passing ideas from
their head to other people - and to agents. Yet kids today are rarely
trained on it, and the market offers drills with streaks, not games with
worlds. Type Space starts as an exciting, well-written game whose
progression *is* keyboard mastery - for a kid or an adult.

**Stage two: thinking in state machines.** Beyond raw speed, the game
teaches hotkeys and the mechanics of building state machines (FSMs)
fast - and with them, a way of organizing thought. Our bet: a player who
can sketch an FSM from the keyboard without breaking flow has
internalized the native shape of a well-designed agent task - states,
transitions, done-conditions. That bet is a hypothesis the prototype
exists to test.

**Stage three: cooperative creation.** Type Space becomes a creativity
tool and a social network with one radical rule - content can only be
born from at least two people, creating dynamically, online, together,
through the FSM-keyboard interface, with agents helping. Every piece
carries provenance: which parts were agent-assisted, which are 100%
human-written. Making that provenance trustworthy rather than merely
claimed is a hard technical problem - we treat it as a core design
constraint from day one, not a marketing line. So is safety: a real-time
creative network that includes kids is a moderation and child-safety
operation, and we weigh that constraint as heavily as the provenance
one.

## Why it's needed

The agent age raises the value of a human who can articulate fast and
structure clearly - the opposite of what "the AI will type for you"
suggests. Solo training exists (barely, joylessly); what does not exist
is the arc from finger skill to structured thought to creating *with*
others. Stage three is the cooperation-first commitment made radical:
where every other creative tool optimizes the solo creator, Type Space
makes another human a precondition of creation. And its provenance
guarantee answers the question every reader, teacher, and parent now
asks: *which of this came from a person?*

## Why not X

**TypingClub / keybr / Monkeytype** - drills and statistics, honest
ones. Typing speed (WPM) goes up; nothing else happens. No world, no
progression of meaning, and the arc ends where Type Space begins.

**Epistory / The Textorcist** - real games, and proof the genre can be
beautiful. But typing is the combat gimmick, the game ends at speed, and
nothing leads onward - no FSM thinking, no agents, no creating together.

**Duolingo-style gamification** - streaks and leagues bolted onto
exercises. Retention mechanics are not game design, and they teach
compliance, not mastery.
