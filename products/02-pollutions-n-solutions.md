# Pollutions & Solutions

**A problem-first way to run any collaborative process: anyone may report
what hurts (a pollution), anyone may propose an answer (a solution), and
authority touches only the final decision.**

## What it is

A methodology, not a tool. A **pollution** is a problem described as a
problem - what is observed, who it affects, what it costs to leave
unfixed, and how you will know it is gone. Never a prescribed fix. A
**solution** is a concrete proposal that answers one or more pollutions,
owned end-to-end by one accountable person, discussed openly, and decided
by whatever rule the group already uses - a lead, a consensus, a vote.
The method does not dictate who decides; it shrinks what the decider
controls. Saying "this hurts" requires no authority, and neither does
proposing an answer.

## Why it's needed

Every collaborative process concentrates power somewhere, and the most
invisible concentration is agenda-setting: whoever frames the problem has
already half-chosen the solution. Pollutions & Solutions splits that
power three ways - the reporter owns the observation, the proposer owns
the answer, the decider owns only the verdict - so cooperation starts at
the framing, not after it. In the agent age this matters even more: agents
can flood a team with plausible proposed changes. Problem-first keeps
humans owning what hurts and what matters, while agents help draft the
answers.

And it is not a software methodology. A family deciding its rules, a
school, a co-op, a city block - we believe the split applies anywhere
problems and authority live together, and the methodology write-up will
test that belief beyond software. Funarchy already governs itself with it:
[the amendment process](../03-amendment-process.md) is
Pollutions & Solutions applied to the organization's own rules.

## Why not X

**Scrum / Jira-style process** - manages tasks and velocity, not
problems. Backlog items are prescriptions ("implement X"), the problem
behind them is rarely written down, and the rituals run on a fixed power
structure. It earns its keep where contracts demand predictable output
and auditable process - and it will still not tell you what actually
hurts.

**Suggestion boxes and forums** - a way to speak with no path to a
result. Problems get reported and upvoted, then rot; nothing binds an
answer to them and nobody owns an outcome. P&S is a suggestion box where every entry has acceptance
criteria and a path to a decided, recorded resolution.

**RFC processes** - solution-first by construction: you must arrive with
a design to participate. That gates participation by design skill and
hands agenda power to whoever writes first. P&S lets "this hurts" stand
alone as a full contribution.
