# Carved Domains: Worked Examples

**Status:** illustrations, not plans. No domain exists until it is
carved. The names below are fictional; the products named are simply
where today's real work lives, so they are the likeliest gravity -
not a prediction, and not a list to fill in.

How carving works is defined in the
[charter](01-charter.md) (Federated Phase) and the
[roadmap](03-roadmap.md) (Domain Carving); the pipeline every carve
travels is the [amendment process](04-amendment-process.md). This
document only shows what those rules look like when lived.

Every example follows the same arc, because every carve does: a
pollution (something hurts), a carve proposal (a solution showing the
three test points), a decision, and a new map.

---

## Example 1: The scenepad carve (first carve - delegation)

**The situation.** Mira joins in the fall on the employer-contract
track and gravitates to scenepad. Over several months she drives the
extraction into a standalone monorepo, cuts releases, writes the
docs. An outside contributor, Toma, starts landing regular pull
requests. scenepad has become a place where two people work every
week.

**The pollution.** Filed in the public forum: "scenepad decisions
wait on the Founder. Releases slip while review queues. The person
who knows the codebase best cannot approve its own roadmap." Who it
affects: Mira, Toma, anyone waiting on a release. Cost of not
fixing: slower releases, a demotivated maintainer, a Founder
bottleneck that will only worsen.

**The carve proposal.** A solution proposes carving a **scenepad
domain** out of technology. The proposal shows the three test
points:

1. *Sustained real work:* months of extraction, releases, and docs,
   all visible in the public record.
2. *More than one person:* Mira and Toma, both active weekly.
3. *An accepted lead:* Mira is willing; Toma explicitly supports.

The proposed text defines the domain's scope (the scenepad monorepo,
its releases, its roadmap), its lead (Mira), and how the lead is
replaced (chosen by the domain's active contributors, confirmed at
the decision step of the current phase).

**The decision.** Two-week discussion window, then the Founders
approve with written rationale. The amendment lands in the public
record and is recorded in `amendment-log.md`.

**The map after.**

| Domain | Lead |
|--------|------|
| scenepad | Mira |
| technology (founding, minus scenepad) | the Founder |
| operations (founding) | the Partner |

scenepad-internal decisions are now Mira's, with team input.
Cross-domain decisions still go to the Founders - one carved domain
is delegation, not federation. The phase does not change. Per the
treasury policy, Mira is added as a multi-sig signer (2-of-3).

## Example 2: The community carve (second carve - federation begins)

**The situation.** After launch the public forum grows. Dana, first
a contributor and later a cooperator, is already doing the work
nobody assigned: moderating discussions, triaging incoming
pollutions, welcoming newcomers, following up on stale threads. Rui
shares the moderation load in another timezone.

**The pollution.** "Community work is real but invisible. It has no
owner, so it competes with everything else for Founder attention -
and loses. Newcomers wait days for a reply." Who it affects: every
newcomer, every open thread, the Founders' focus. Cost of not
fixing: the forum decays exactly when growth needs it most.

**The carve proposal.** A **community domain**: scope is the health
of the public forum, contributor onboarding, and outreach. The test:

1. *Sustained real work:* months of moderation and triage, visible
   in the forum's own history.
2. *More than one person:* Dana and Rui.
3. *An accepted lead:* Dana, with Rui's support on the proposal.

**The decision.** The phase decider at that moment is still the
Founders (one carved domain so far). Discussion, then approval with
rationale; recorded in `amendment-log.md`.

**The map after.**

| Domain | Lead |
|--------|------|
| scenepad | Mira |
| community | Dana |
| technology (founding, minus carves) | the Founder |
| operations (founding, minus carves) | the Partner |

This is the second carved domain, so **the Federated Phase begins
with this approval** - the switch is the event, not the calendar.
The change is concrete: the next amendment that reaches its decision
step is decided by consensus of Mira, Dana, and the Founders - not
by the Founders alone. Dana joins the multi-sig; the threshold is
adjusted (2-of-4).

## Example 3: The carve that fails the test

**The situation.** Rei, newly arrived and full of energy, proposes
an **AI research domain**: "Funarchy should explore agent
architectures beyond its products. Make me research lead and I will
build the effort."

**Why it fails.** Held against the three test points at the decision
step:

1. *Sustained real work:* none yet - the proposal describes work
   that would begin after approval.
2. *More than one person:* only Rei.
3. *An accepted lead:* there is nobody to do the accepting.

**The decision.** Rejected, calmly, with written rationale: "A
domain is carved around work that already happens; it is never a
license to begin. Nothing here needs permission - start the
research, publish it in the public record, find the people who care.
If the work becomes real and shared, propose the carve again and it
will likely pass."

**What it shows.** The test has teeth, and rejection is not
punishment. Starting work needs no domain, no title, and no
approval - that is Permissionlessness. The domain, if it ever
exists, is recognition of work, not the cause of it.

## What these examples are not

- Not a roadmap: none of these carves is planned or promised.
- Not a list to complete: the map grows only as real work demands,
  and may grow in directions no example here anticipates.
- Not binding: if an example here ever disagrees with the charter,
  bylaws, or roadmap, those documents govern.
