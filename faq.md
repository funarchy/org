# FAQ: the recurring objections

Written answers to the objections that recur in first-contact
conversations. Each answer is grounded in the documents and meant to
be deliverable in a sentence — the long version is one link away.
Part of the solution to pollution #4; more objections land here as
more conversations happen.

## "So this is a charity?"

No — a workers' cooperative. Members are paid for their labor,
comparable to or above market rates
([charter, Non-Exploitation](01-charter.md)); revenue comes from
SaaS, consulting, and donations ([roadmap](02-roadmap.md)). What
differs from a company is not the absence of money — it is where
control and surplus go: decision power is never tied to investment,
and surplus is reinvested, never paid out as dividends.

## "How does this get funded, if not by investment?"

By deliberately diversified revenue: SaaS, consulting, grants,
donations — so that no single funder can buy control
([charter, Sovereignty](01-charter.md)). Funders advise; members
decide. This model is not exotic: donation-funded foundations employ
the maintainers of some of the most-used software in the world — see
[research/ownership-models.md](research/ownership-models.md).

## "Whom does this displace?"

Rented, closed coordination machinery — not people. RPPS replaces
the Jira/Linear layer: process tooling that describes work without
governing it ([products/04-rpps.md](products/04-rpps.md), "Why not
X"); Funarchy Space replaces the per-seat private cockpit with a
team-owned environment ([products/03-space.md](products/03-space.md)).
The people who run coordination today keep the part of the job that
was always human — ownership and decisions stay named and visible;
what disappears is the machinery that substituted for cooperation
([products/README.md](products/README.md)).

## "Nobody works without being paid."

Agreed — and nobody here is asked to. Paid membership is the model,
not volunteering: members receive fair compensation for their labor
([charter, Non-Exploitation](01-charter.md)). Contribution before
membership is possible because everything is open
([charter, Permissionlessness](01-charter.md)), but it is a path to
paid membership ([onboarding](05-onboarding-guide.md)), not a
substitute for it.

## "How much do you expect to spend on marketing?"

Zero on advertising and paid user acquisition. Users are earned, not
bought: the value itself is open — organized processes, public
records, freely available learning materials
([charter, Openness](01-charter.md)) — and the rest is community
work: speaking with people, explaining the idea, teaching. The
hundreds-of-dollars-per-user economics of SaaS marketing is a cost
of being closed; an open record is its own distribution.
